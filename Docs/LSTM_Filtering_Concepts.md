# LSTM Prediction Filtering Concepts for Trading Strategies

This document outlines various filtering concepts that can be applied to the raw price predictions generated by an LSTM model. The goal of these filters is to enhance the robustness and reliability of trading signals derived from the LSTM's output, especially when integrating it into a separate, rule-based trading strategy.

This LSTM model (LSTM Tool V1.0) is developed and trained in the [applied-ml-trading-lstm-eurusd](https://github.com/ilahuerta-IA/applied-ml-trading-lstm-eurusd) repository. The implementation and backtesting of these filtering concepts will occur in the separate [trading-strategy-optimizer](https://github.com/ilahuerta-IA/trading-strategy-optimizer.git) repository, which handles the strategy logic and simulation.

Remember, for each of these filters, the LSTM Tool V1.0 model will be making a prediction for `Close[t+1]` (the next 5-minute bar's close price) based on its `WINDOW` of past 'Close' prices. You'd then use this `Predicted_Close[t+1]` and/or derived signals from it.

---

## 1. Magnitude-Based Filters (Leveraging Prediction Strength)

These filters utilize the *strength* or *size* of the LSTM's predicted price movement.

### 1.1. Minimum Predicted Move Threshold

*   **Idea:** Don't just confirm direction; confirm that the LSTM predicts a significant enough move. Filter out strategy signals where the LSTM only predicts a tiny price change, as these are often noise or within typical spread/slippage costs. Focus on higher-conviction signals.
*   **How to use:**
    *   Calculate the predicted change: `Predicted_Delta = Predicted_Close[t+1] - Actual_Close[t]`.
    *   **Rule (for LONG confirmation):** If the trading strategy signals LONG, AND `Predicted_Delta` > `min_pip_threshold_for_long` (e.g., 0.5 pips, 1 pip).
    *   **Rule (for SHORT confirmation):** If the trading strategy signals SHORT, AND `Predicted_Delta` < `-min_pip_threshold_for_short`.
*   **Contribution:** Reduces noise, avoids trades where the predicted move is too small to cover transaction costs, and focuses on signals with more predicted "follow-through."
*   **Complexity:** Low.

### 1.2. Volatility-Adjusted Predicted Move (Adaptive Threshold)

*   **Idea:** Make the `min_pip_threshold` (above) adaptive to current market volatility. In volatile markets, 1 pip is small; in quiet markets, it's large.
*   **How to use:**
    *   Calculate `Predicted_Delta = Predicted_Close[t+1] - Actual_Close[t]`.
    *   Calculate a volatility measure, such as Average True Range (ATR) or the standard deviation of recent price changes (e.g., over the last 14-20 periods).
    *   **Rule (for LONG confirmation):** If the trading strategy signals LONG, AND `Predicted_Delta` > `X * ATR[t]` (e.g., `0.1 * ATR[t]`).
    *   **Rule (for SHORT confirmation):** If the trading strategy signals SHORT, AND `Predicted_Delta` < `-X * ATR[t]`.
*   **Contribution:** Dynamically filters signals based on current market volatility, potentially leading to more appropriate trade entries and improved risk management.
*   **Complexity:** Medium (requires calculating ATR/volatility, but libraries like `talib` or `Pandas` rolling functions make this easy).

## 2. Price Level & Range Filters (Addressing "Wild" Predictions)

These filters check if the LSTM's predicted price level is "reasonable" or aligns with certain boundaries, helping to filter out potentially erroneous "wild" predictions.

### 2.1. Predicted Price within a "Reasonable" Band

*   **Idea:** Filter out LSTM predictions that suggest extremely large or radical price movements, as these are often errors or indicate highly unusual, unpredictable market events.
*   **How to use:**
    *   Define a "reasonable" price band around the `Actual_Close[t]` or a central moving average (e.g., SMA50 of price).
    *   **Rule (for trade confirmation):** If the trading strategy signals a trade, AND `Predicted_Close[t+1]` is **within** `Actual_Close[t] +/- Y_pips` (e.g., 5-10 pips), THEN consider the LSTM signal valid for confirmation.
    *   **Rule (for signal invalidation - Inverse):** If `Predicted_Close[t+1]` is *outside* `Actual_Close[t] +/- Y_pips`, THEN invalidate the trading strategy's signal, regardless of other conditions. This acts as a protective filter against outlier LSTM predictions.
*   **Contribution:** Prevents the strategy from reacting to "outlier" or "wild" LSTM predictions, adding a safety layer and reducing risk from model errors.
*   **Complexity:** Low.

### 2.2. Predicted Crossover Confirmation

*   **Idea:** Use the LSTM to predict if the price will cross a significant technical level (like a Moving Average), adding a layer of "leading" confirmation to your strategy.
*   **How to use:**
    *   Calculate a key technical indicator for price (e.g., SMA50) for the current bar: `SMA50[t]`.
    *   **Rule (for LONG confirmation):** If the trading strategy signals LONG, AND `Actual_Close[t]` is currently below `SMA50[t]`, BUT `Predicted_Close[t+1]` is predicted to be *above* `SMA50[t]`, THEN confirm. (You might also consider `SMA50[t+1]` if you can reasonably extrapolate it).
    *   **Rule (for SHORT confirmation):** If the trading strategy signals SHORT, AND `Actual_Close[t]` is currently above `SMA50[t]`, BUT `Predicted_Close[t+1]` is predicted to be *below* `SMA50[t]`.
*   **Contribution:** Adds a layer of "leading" confirmation by having the LSTM predict a specific market event (a crossover) rather than just a general direction.
*   **Complexity:** Medium (requires calculating the technical indicator, which you will do for the trading strategy).

## 3. Consensus & Smoothing Filters (Refining Directional Agreement)

These filters enhance the reliability of the LSTM's directional signal by requiring agreement over multiple predictions or smoothing the prediction line itself. Your "X out of Y" idea fits well here.

### 3.1. Smoothed LSTM Prediction Line Direction (as an Indicator)

*   **Idea:** Instead of relying on a single raw `Predicted_Close[t+1]`, apply a short moving average (e.g., SMA3 or EMA5) *to the sequence of raw LSTM predictions themselves*. Then, use the direction of this *smoothed prediction line* for confirmation.
*   **How to use:**
    *   Maintain a history of `Raw_LSTM_Predictions` (e.g., the last 5 or 10 raw predictions).
    *   Calculate `Smoothed_LSTM_Prediction_Line[t]` = `SMA(Raw_LSTM_Predictions_History, N)` (e.g., N=3 or N=5).
    *   **Rule (for LONG confirmation):** If the trading strategy signals LONG, AND `Smoothed_LSTM_Prediction_Line[t]` > `Smoothed_LSTM_Prediction_Line[t-1]`, THEN confirm.
    *   **Rule (for SHORT confirmation):** If the trading strategy signals SHORT, AND `Smoothed_LSTM_Prediction_Line[t]` < `Smoothed_LSTM_Prediction_Line[t-1]`.
*   **Contribution:** Creates a more robust and less volatile LSTM indicator. Filters out jerky, one-off LSTM predictions, requiring sustained LSTM conviction.
*   **Complexity:** Low (requires keeping a short history of LSTM predictions and applying a simple average).

### 3.2. Weighted Directional Agreement (Refinement of "X out of Y")

*   **Idea:** In your existing "X out of Y" rule (e.g., 8 out of 10 predict up), give more importance to the *most recent* LSTM predictions within the `Y` window.
*   **How to use:**
    *   Assign weights (e.g., linearly decreasing, exponentially decreasing) to the last `Y` LSTM directional predictions. For example, `P_t` (prediction for current bar) gets weight 10, `P_t-1` gets weight 9, etc.
    *   Calculate a `Weighted_Directional_Score` by summing the weighted "votes" (+1 for UP, -1 for DOWN).
    *   **Rule:** If the trading strategy signals, AND `Weighted_Directional_Score` > `weighted_score_threshold`, THEN confirm.
*   **Contribution:** More responsive than a simple X/Y count, as recent predictions have more influence, while still providing a consensus.
*   **Complexity:** Medium.

## 4. Uncertainty/Confidence-Based Filters (More Advanced & Computationally Intensive)

These filters try to use the LSTM's "uncertainty" in its prediction to filter out less reliable signals.

### 4.1. Dropout at Inference (Monte Carlo Dropout)

*   **Idea:** While typically used during training, keeping Dropout layers active during inference (prediction) allows you to run `model.predict()` multiple times for the same input. Each run gives a slightly different prediction. The *variance* of these predictions can be an estimate of the model's uncertainty.
*   **How to use:**
    *   Modify the Keras model (or use a custom function) to ensure Dropout layers are active during prediction (`tf.keras.backend.set_learning_phase(1)` or `model(inputs, training=True)`).
    *   For each bar, run `model.predict(x_input)` `K` times (e.g., K=10 or 20).
    *   Calculate the mean of these `K` predictions as your primary `Predicted_Close[t+1]`.
    *   Calculate the standard deviation (or variance) of these `K` predictions.
    *   **Rule:** If the trading strategy signals, AND the `standard_deviation` of the LSTM's predictions for that bar is `< max_uncertainty_threshold`, THEN confirm.
*   **Contribution:** Filters out predictions where the model itself is "unsure," increasing the conviction of confirmed signals. This taps into the model's internal confidence.
*   **Complexity:** High (computationally intensive at inference time, requires careful implementation in the trading strategy to avoid look-ahead bias).
